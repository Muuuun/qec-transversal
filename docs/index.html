§1Six kinds of “transversal” — four decided here

The word “transversal” names many inequivalent gate classes in the literature, differing in how far a single fault can spread. This zoo decides four of them for every code: strict — one depth-one layer U₁ ⊗ … ⊗ Un of independent single-qubit Cliffords, no two-qubit gates, no permutations (the strongest notion: a fault reaches nothing); T-level — the same shape at the third hierarchy level, ∏ᵢ Tᵢ^tᵢ; fold — single-qubit gates plus CZ across the pairs of a ZX-duality; and automorphism — permutation gates from the code's symmetry group. The first two are decided by kernel computations, complete outright; fold is complete per certified duality; automorphism groups are exact for the given checks.

When a paper says the toric or BB code “has transversal gates”, it means one of the weaker rows below — which is why our strict verdict (“none”) and that folklore are both right:

§4How much of the Clifford group do you actually get?

Having a transversal gate and having all Clifford gates transversally are very different things. The census column “strict group” shows both numbers for every code: the order of the group its transversal gates generate, against the target |Sp(2k,2)| — the full logical Clifford group on its k qubits. Three tiers cover the whole zoo:

2 codes

the full Clifford group

“Full” is always relative to a gate class — the ★ marks it and its tooltip names the class. Steane reaches it with strict gates alone; the surface code only via its fold (its strict gates are trivial); the non-CSS [[5,1,3]] (§6 callout) only in the monomial class.

And the count grows in the multi-matching class. Composing depth-one two-local layers over many matchings (Albert's N2fold; sampled with automorphism-seeded matchings, positive certificates only), more codes certify the full logical Clifford group: grid-4x6 — all of Sp(16,2), order ~6×1040, independently confirming Albert's census — plus iceberg-8 (Sp(12,2)), cube-832 (Sp(6,2)), and c6-22 / c4-22 (Sp(4,2)); two Steane blocks likewise certify full Sp(4,2) via the inter-block pairing. Both encode a single logical qubit; that is no accident, since the transversal gates act globally and k = 1 leaves nothing to address individually. These are the ★ points on the map.

38 codes

a thin global slice

Every other code with gates gets a small, fixed subgroup: order 6 — or 48 on the symmetric BB codes — out of a target that grows like 2k². The gross code's fold gates cover 6 of ~1090. The gates are collective: one S̄, H̄, or all-pairs CZ̄ hitting every logical qubit at once. Addressing a single logical qubit transversally is essentially never possible — that is what lattice surgery, automorphism circuits, and teleportation are for.

0 codes

universality — forbidden

No code has a universal transversal set; the Eastin–Knill theorem rules it out for every code with distance ≥ 2. The zoo shows the forced trade: QRM15 buys a transversal T̄ at the price of its Clifford group (order 2, no transversal H̄), while Steane has the whole Clifford group but no T̄. Every code sits on one side of this line.

Beyond CSS, beyond one qubit per cell

The engines behind this page now decide four classes for any stabilizer code, CSS or not. Three results that do not fit the census table:

The [[5,1,3]] perfect code (non-CSS): its strict-transversal group is the cyclic C₃ = ⟨(SH)⊗5⟩, and its full monomial group (order 360) realizes the complete logical Clifford group — both computed exactly by the general-stabilizer solver and verified against 6⁵ brute force.

Steane, seen whole: the monomial group is 1008 = 6 local-Clifford × |PGL(3,2)| = 168 permutations — the full symmetry that check-basis encodings hide.

Two-local layers change the game: pairing [[4,2,2]]'s qubits (0,1)(2,3) lifts its logical gate group from order 6 to 48; on the toric code's fold matching the complete two-local group turns out to equal the diagonal-plus-fold-Hadamard group already certified — the fold gates were everything.

§6The codes with strict gates: exact solutions

The exact solutions. Each filled strip is a parameter vector: apply √Z (or √X) on the filled qubits. These are the classical positive controls — self-dual or dual-containing CSS codes — and none of them is LDPC: their gates come from algebraic structure (doubly-even self-duality, Reed–Muller nesting) that check-sparsity destroys.

So which code with gates is “best”? Three answers

By encoding rate k/n alone: the iceberg family [[2m, 2m−2, 2]] — iceberg-12 = [[12,10,2]] holds the zoo record at rate 5/6, and the family reaches rate → 1. But distance is frozen at 2: these codes only detect errors. Raw rate is a misleading scoreboard.

By the operational figure of merit kd²/n (surface code ≈ 1, the qldpc-challenge metric): the middle Reed–Muller family wins decisively — rm256 = [[256,70,16]] scores kd²/n = 70 and rm64 = [[64,20,8]] scores 20, versus 12 for the gross code, 13.5 for two-gross, and ≤ 24.5 for bb756. In this family kd²/n = C(m, m/2) is unbounded — but the checks are dense (weight 32–256 at n = 256), so the LDPC property is the price of admission.

Within LDPC and growing distance: nothing strict — every qLDPC family here is certified strictly gate-free. The certified fold layer softens this only a little: the fold groups we certify are small (order 6, or 48 on symmetric BB codes) — global S̄/H̄-type actions, far short of the full logical Clifford group. High rate, growing distance, sparse checks, and a large transversal-class gate group in one code remains open territory.

§8k = 1 strict fullness — the regime left open by Chakraborty–Gottesman

For one logical qubit the full logical Clifford group mod Paulis is Sp(2,2) ≅ S₃, order 6. Chakraborty–Gottesman (arXiv:2602.13395) close the k ≥ 2 regime with a no-go for strict fullness; k = 1 is the regime their result leaves open. The open question, precisely: characterize all k = 1 stabilizer codes whose strict-transversal group mod Pauli is the full Sp(2,2). One positive family is classical and well known — self-dual doubly-even CSS codes (the 2D color codes, Steane the smallest member) — so nothing here claims novelty for Steane's fullness. What this section adds is certified data points on the question, from the registry and the external census above; it is a data table on an open classification problem, not a new finding.

§9The method, and why a trivial kernel is a proof

A strict-transversal gate applies one single-qubit Clifford to each physical qubit — no two-qubit gates, no permutations — so faults cannot spread inside a block. Write CX, CZ for the row spans of the check matrices and ⊙ for the coordinatewise product. A phase layer UZ(a) = ∏i √Ziai conjugates Xv to Xv Zv⊙a, so it preserves the stabilizer exactly when

AZ = { a ∈ 𝔽₂ⁿ : a ⊙ CX ⊆ CZ },  AX = { b ∈ 𝔽₂ⁿ : b ⊙ CZ ⊆ CX }.

For each check x of HX, the condition a ⊙ x ∈ CZ is the linear system QZ diag(x) aᵀ = 0 with QZ spanning ker HZ. Stacking all checks: AZ = ker MZ. So rank MZ = n proves the kernel is {0}.

Diagonal layers are not the whole story — a transversal gate could mix X and Z (Hadamards, a different Clifford per qubit). The completeness theorem (Albert, arXiv:2608.05688) closes that gap: every strict-transversal Clifford factors as

g = H(t) UZ(q) UX(p),  t ∈ AZ ∩ AX, q ∈ AZ, p ∈ AX, q ⊙ p = 0,

with H(t) = UZ(t)UX(t)UZ(t). Hence AZ = AX = {0} ⇒ the only strict-transversal gates are Paulis.

Exhaustive validation We did not take the theorem on faith. For 46 codes with n ≤ 8 we enumerated all 6ⁿ assignments of arbitrary single-qubit Cliffords (up to 1,679,616 layers for the [[8,2,2]] toric code), kept those preserving the stabilizer, and compared. In every case the brute-force group, the group generated from AZ/AX alone, and the counting formula #{(t,q,p) : q⊙p = 0} agreed exactly. For the toric code the brute force found only the identity — a trivial kernel is a genuine nonexistence proof, not a blind spot of the diagonal search.

Scope of this section. The completeness theorem and validation above cover the strict class. The fold verdicts use the fixed-matching analogue of the same construction — the SMZ/SMX kernels of Albert's framework on a certified ZX-duality (Breuckmann–Burton, arXiv:2202.06647; Eberhardt–Steffan, arXiv:2407.03973) — and the T-level verdicts use the same coset-phase argument lifted from 𝔽₂ to ℤ₈, each with its own kernel certificate.

Don't trust us — check the witnesses. Every strict verdict on this page ships with a machine-checkable witness (docs/zoo/witnesses/, one gzipped JSON per code: constraint rows with their derivations, kernel bases, logical bases, gate actions, and full group element lists) and an independent checker — a standalone ~200-line script, numpy only, importing nothing from this project, with its own Gaussian elimination. It re-verifies soundness and completeness of every verdict from first principles and is mutation-tested (nine classes of forged witness, all rejected):

python tools/check_witness.py docs/zoo/witnesses/*.json.gz   # 42/42 PASS
